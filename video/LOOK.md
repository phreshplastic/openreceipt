# Visual and sound direction

The film should feel authored, quiet, and physical. The product remains the visual subject: large interface crops, real cursor movement, paper texture, and the printer mechanism carry the interest while titles explain only what the captured state cannot say by itself.

## Frame language

Use full-bleed interface footage for proof, then crop to the receipt or tool call when the narration names it. Keep the active detail inside the center 80% so it remains readable on a phone. A crop should settle before the important action and hold afterward; constant camera drift makes the interaction harder to verify.

Move between the digital and physical versions through visual matches: the tall white receipt on-screen cuts to the tall white receipt emerging from the printer, and the approval button cuts to the printer waking. Prefer hard cuts, short dissolves, and motivated pushes. Avoid decorative wipes, synthetic depth, floating glass cards, and generated B-roll because they make a working product look like a concept render.

The optional founder shot earns its place only if a clean six-to-eight-second take makes the premise faster to understand. Use a full-frame desk shot, not a persistent face bubble.

## Color and type

Use the app's own neutral palette so the edit and captured UI belong together:

- Ground `#F5F5F7`, paper `#FFFFFF`, ink `#1D1D1F`, muted text `#6E6E73`, and product blue `#0071E3`.
- Let warm practical light and the cream tone of real thermal paper supply the warmth. Avoid Anthropic coral or another company's signature palette.
- Use Inter for editorial titles and the captured receipt's monospace face for measurements or tool-call details. Keep title copy in sentence case, usually six words or fewer.

Titles should sit near the evidence they describe and disappear once the viewer has read them. Tool names may use monospace, but they should read as annotations rather than terminal cosplay.

## Motion

At 30 fps, use 12-20 frames for short crops or position changes and roughly 24-36 frames for a deliberate camera push. Use the product easing curve `cubic-bezier(0.23, 1, 0.32, 1)` as the default. Cursor movement comes from the real capture; editorial motion should direct attention around it rather than compete with it.

Give named tools, the preview dimensions, and the approval panel at least 1.5 seconds of settled readability. The approval panel deserves the longest hold because it proves the safety model. Use one faster montage during the Lisbon draft, then slow down from preview through physical print so the ending feels consequential.

## Sound

Open on the printer motor alone for about one second, then bring in jazz underneath the first line. Duck the music whenever narration explains WebMCP, preview, or approval, and let the real printer rise again during the match cut. Aim for a final program near YouTube's common playback target of -14 LUFS integrated with peaks below -1 dBTP, but preserve natural speech dynamics rather than chasing the meter.

No transition needs a synthetic whoosh by default. Paper handling, button contact, printer startup, and the motor provide a more specific sound vocabulary.

## Still-review test

Every representative frame should answer one question immediately: what changed, what tool acted, what the person approved, or what became physical. Reject a frame if the relevant UI cannot be read at phone size, the title repeats the narration without locating evidence, or the treatment could belong to any generic AI product.
