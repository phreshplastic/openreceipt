For a new OpenReceipt setup, I would start with an **80 mm phenol-free roll** that fits the printer manual. Choose 58 mm when the narrow paper is part of the appeal. Three details matter: **width**, **roll size**, and **coating**.

## Width: 80 mm or 58 mm

Measure the printer, not the receipt you imagine.

- **80 mm** feels like the full-size slip a shop hands you. On the Epson TM-L90 it gives you 576 printable dots across, which lets tables and small charts breathe. OpenReceipt uses this as the default profile.
- **58 mm** feels closer to a note or label. OpenReceipt draws it at 420 dots across. Checklists and reminders read well; wide tables feel tight.

The TM-L90 ships as an 80 mm printer and takes 58 mm rolls with a plastic spacer that clips into the paper bay. The app has a real 58 mm profile, so the preview matches the narrower roll exactly — pick the width in setup and the editor reflows to the printer’s dot width.

Do not buy 58 mm rolls for a printer without the spacer, and do not force an 80 mm roll into a 58 mm printer. The roll has to sit straight for the cutter to work.

## Roll size: diameter and length

Listings quote width, outer diameter, and sometimes length. Check the printer’s maximum roll diameter before ordering. Two rolls with the same outer size can hold different lengths because paper thickness and core size vary.

## Weight and coating

Paper weight mostly changes how the receipt feels. Heavier stock can feel nicer for a note someone will keep, but GSM measures mass rather than quality. Coatings and paper fibers change the relationship between weight and thickness, and the printer manual sets the thickness it can feed and cut.

The coating is worth reading about because traditional thermal papers use a chemical developer. The European Chemicals Agency says the EU restricted **BPA** in thermal paper in January 2020. It also reports that manufacturers commonly replaced BPA with **BPS**, another bisphenol that raises reproductive and hormonal concerns.

That is why labels such as **phenol-free**, **BPA/BPS-free**, or **developer-free** tell you more than BPA-free alone. Koehler says its blue [Blue4est](https://www.koehlerpaper.com/en/products/Thermal-paper/TH_Blue4est_POS_cash-register-rolls.php) paper forms an image without a chemical color developer. Its receipt stock comes in 48 and 52 gsm versions. Whatever you choose, match its thickness and roll diameter to the printer.

## Storage

Thermal paper is light- and heat-sensitive. Keep spare rolls in a drawer, not on a sunny shelf, and away from anything warm — including the top of the printer itself. A roll that has gone gray or yellow at the edges will still print, but the background will not be white.

## What we use

80 mm phenol-free rolls, 80 × 80 mm, in the TM-L90 on the desk — one in the printer, two in the drawer. The 58 mm profile is there when a narrower receipt suits the thing being printed. Both profiles are one tap apart in the app’s print settings.
